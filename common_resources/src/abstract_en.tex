\section*{Abstract}
\vskip 0.5em

This bachelor's thesis presents a method for classifying Czech articles by their journalistic quality. We used \ac{NLP} and \ac{ML} techniques to build a multi-class classification system. The system was trained on a dataset from Seznam.cz, an online Czech platform, which includes article texts and quality labels given by human editors. Articles were grouped into four quality levels (Insufficient, Short but Sufficient, Standard, High-Quality) based on editorial standards. We tested several models, including a fine-tuned \ac{DeBERTaV3} transformer and a \ac{CatBoost} decision tree classifier with custom features. We also developed a hybrid model combining \ac{DeBERTaV3} outputs with \ac{CatBoost}, using both text representations and metadata. The hybrid model achieved the best performance. On a held-out test set, the final system reached a weighted F1 score of 0.78. This result is close to the agreement between human editors and demonstrates the effectiveness of our approach. The results show that advanced language models, when combined with language and metadata features, can reliably predict article quality in Czech media. This work provides a solid basis for future practical tools to automatically assess journalistic quality and improve content management. Such tools could help editors filter and prioritize content by quality.
